ClimaLand · model description

A prognostic live-carbon model without plant functional types

Four live carbon pools driven by an existing photosynthesis and leaf-area model, closing the plant and soil carbon budgets that were previously open — and a measured account of where a no-PFT formulation stops working.

Branch ar/prognostic_carbon PR #1834 20-site ERA5 column battery

This describes a carbon model added to ClimaLand's canopy: four prognostic pools — labile sugar, leaf, stem and root — plus a prognostic soil organic carbon pool that was previously frozen. It is written against results from a 20-site single-column battery spanning tropical rainforest to tundra, and it reports what the model gets right and what it does not.

The governing design rule is that the carbon model does not change GPP or leaf area index. Both are already modelled — a P-model for photosynthesis, and either prescribed MODIS LAI or a prognostic optimal-LAI scheme — and the carbon model consumes them as inputs. This one-way coupling is what makes the model cheap to spin up and its correctness checkable: a run with the pools must reproduce a run without them exactly.

§1

The pools and their fluxes

All pools are in kg C m⁻² of ground and live in the canopy's prognostic state. Gross primary production arrives as sugar; sugar pays maintenance respiration and funds structural growth; structure turns over into litter.

dC_sugar/dt = GPP − Rm − S dC_leaf/dt = a·f_leaf·S − C_leaf/τ_leaf dC_stem/dt = a·f_stem·S − C_stem/τ_stem dC_root/dt = a·f_root·S − C_root/τ_root
S
sugar drawn for structure (kg C m⁻² s⁻¹)
a
construction efficiency, 0.7; the remaining (1−a)·S is growth respiration
f_leaf, f_stem, f_root
allocation fractions summing to one, blended between C3 and C4 values by the modelled C3 fraction
τ_i
turnover times: leaf 1.5 yr, root 2 yr, stem 30 yr (C3) or 1 yr (C4)

Summing the four gives d(ΣC)/dt = GPP − Ra − litter, with Ra = Rm + (1−a)S. That identity is the conservation test: it closes only if growth respiration is exactly the construction cost and allocation is neither double-counted nor dropped.

Why sugar never pins at zero

The allocation rate, not the fractions, regulates the sugar pool. Sugar is treated as a buffer with a target set by standing live biomass, and drawn down through a smooth ramp:

C_sugar_target = c_nsc·(C_leaf + C_sap + C_root) x = C_sugar / C_sugar_target S = (C_sugar/τ_alloc)·g(x), g(x) = xⁿ/(1 + xⁿ), n = 3

Above target the ramp saturates and growth draws the pool down; below target growth stops while maintenance continues. That is a proportional controller, so sugar oscillates seasonally around its target rather than saturating or emptying — and it needs no hard clamp. Genuine carbon starvation remains something the model can express rather than a numerical failure.

Maintenance respiration

This replaces a JULES-style term that respired prescribed constant stem and root area indices. With real pools, the pools are what respire:

Rm = g(C_sugar/C_sugar_ref)·[ f_T·(M_C·Rd + r_stem·C_sap) + f_T·r_root·C_root ] C_sap = C_stem/(1 + C_stem/C_sap_half) living sapwood fraction f_T = Q10^((T − T_ref)/10)

Three choices carry weight here. The leaf term reuses Rd, the dark respiration the photosynthesis scheme already computes, which avoids double-counting it — it is already inside net assimilation. The sapwood saturation is what stops a 20 kg C m⁻² tropical stem pool from respiring itself to death, since living wood does not scale linearly with total wood. And the leading ramp is a substrate limitation: respiration requires substrate, so it stops as the sugar pool empties rather than driving it negative.

Measured, not assumed

That last term was added after the battery produced C_sugar = −0.075 kg C m⁻² at the Sahara column, where GPP is zero all year and maintenance kept drawing on an empty pool. Negative carbon is not a physical outcome. With the ramp, the pool floors at exactly zero and vegetated sites move by at most one part in 10⁴.

§2

Respiration that a desert can switch off

The scheme this replaces respired prescribed area indices, which do not vanish over bare ground. Before the change, the two true desert columns reported 0.813 g C m⁻² day⁻¹ of autotrophic respiration with zero photosynthesis and zero leaf area, all year — carbon emitted that was never fixed, from a pool that did not exist. Two further sites respired more than they assimilated, giving negative annual net production.

The prediction was recorded before the run: if respiration comes from the pools, desert Ra must fall to zero, because the pools there equilibrate at zero. It does, exactly — and no site now respires more than it assimilates.

A parameter that was quietly inert

The legacy scheme's temperature sensitivity ships at Q10 = 1.0, so f_T ≡ 1 and maintenance had no temperature response at all. Across a 38 K span — Alaska at 262 K to the Sahel at 300 K, where Q10 = 2 predicts a 14× range — measured Ra varied by ±3%. The carbon model therefore carries its own Q10 = 2.0, deliberately separate, so the legacy scheme's behaviour is untouched and the change is visible rather than smuggled in.

§3

Closing the soil budget

Soil organic carbon already existed as a prognostic variable but was pinned at its initial condition: microbial respiration added CO₂ and consumed O₂ but never drew SOC down. The soil carbon budget was open in exactly the way the plant budget had been.

dSOC/dt = I_litter(z) − Sm I_litter(z) = (L_leaf + L_stem)·s(z)/∫s + L_root·r(z)/∫r

Root litter follows the root profile; leaf and stem litter enter on a shallow exponential. Each shape is normalised by its own column integral, so the integral of the input returns the surface litter flux exactly, whatever the vertical discretisation.

Why the normalisation is load-bearing

On the standard column, a 5 cm e-folding depth against a 5 cm top layer leaves the raw shape integrating to 0.892. An un-normalised input would have delivered 11% less carbon to the soil than the canopy shed — silently. The test asserts both that the column integral returns the litter flux to round-off and that the raw shape does not integrate to one, so the normalisation cannot be dropped without a failure.

Across the battery, SOC drifts between −4.7% and +0.3% over two years with mixed signs — the signature of a system near balance rather than a systematic bias — and heterotrophic respiration stays within 3% of its baseline everywhere. The respiration ratio tracks the SOC ratio nearly one-to-one, which confirms the feedback is live where SOC was previously frozen.

§4

Equilibrium, reached offline

Stem carbon turns over on a 30-year timescale and soil carbon on centuries, so no coupled run reaches steady state. Because the coupling is one-way, the pool equations can be integrated alone against a recorded driver series — GPP, leaf respiration, canopy temperature, C3 fraction — for centuries in seconds rather than node-hours.

Two independent methods agree. A 600-year explicit integration and a direct steady-state solution match to ratio 1.000 on all structural pools under constant drivers. The direct solution is not closed-form: at equilibrium the pools are linear in the mean allocation rate, but maintenance respiration depends on the sapwood it builds, so the rate is a scalar fixed point solved by bisection.

C_i* = a·f_i·S̄·τ_i linear in the mean allocation rate S̄ = GPP − Rm(S̄) fixed point: Rm depends on the pools it builds

§5

Where a no-PFT formulation stops

Because equilibrium stem carbon is linear in turnover time, the battery states plainly what turnover time each site would need to hit its target. The answer is physically coherent — and it is where the approach runs out.

Scaling turnover time by mean annual temperature, τ_stem · q^((T_ref − MAT)/10) with q = 2 and T_ref = 283 K, fixes the cold end completely and leaves the warm end untouched:

An effective turnover of 107 years at central Siberia is a real boreal number rather than a fitted one, and the scaling is flat above the reference temperature so warm sites are untouched by construction. But grasslands are warm, so nothing reaches them.

This is now in the model, driven by a running mean of air temperature the carbon model carries itself with two years of memory. Adding that fifth prognostic variable leaves GPP and leaf area index bit-identical across all 20 sites, and the coupled run shifts cold sites in temperature order while moving warm sites by exactly zero.

What the coupled battery cannot show

The table above is an equilibrium result. Over the two years a coupled column run spans, stem carbon is set by growth rather than turnover — τ(1−e−t/τ) differs by only 2.4% between 107 and 30 years. The battery confirms the model still runs, that the one-way coupling still holds exactly, and that the multiplier lands where it should; it does not and cannot demonstrate the biomass change itself. A green 20-site run is not evidence for the fix.

Precipitation does not rescue it either

The obvious second climate axis fails for a specific and instructive reason. Among the four sites that matter, the wettest is a grassland and the two driest are forests:

Any woody fraction that increases with precipitation therefore suppresses the boreal forests 2–3× harder than the grassland it is meant to fix. Measured: it bought one grassland site and cost one forest site, while cutting central Siberia's stem pool from 2.07 to 0.20 kg C m⁻².

The honest limit

Pampas is a fire- and grazing-maintained grassland sitting in a climate that would support forest. That is not a climate fact, so no function of mean annual temperature and precipitation can encode it.

Soil properties were tested next and do not carry it either. Sand, coarse fragments and porosity all overlap between grassland and forest. Organic matter separates them by 10% — but the US Great Plains grassland and a central European forest have near-identical sand fractions (0.162 vs 0.161); a boreal forest that must stay woody sits only 16% above pampas; and tundra, which has no trees, carries the highest organic matter of the whole temperate–boreal group. Organic matter is tracking cold, wet, slow decomposition — peat — not woodiness.

So across both climate and soil, nothing available separates a disturbance-maintained grassland from a forest in the same climate on the same soil. This is a real property of the problem rather than a gap in the search: the distinction is fire and grazing history. A PFT-free model can capture the cold-forest end and the arid end well; it over-builds fire-maintained grassland, and that limitation should be documented rather than tuned away.

§6

Against observed woody biomass

The equilibrium woody pool can be compared with satellite and inventory biomass products directly. Six are available: XuSaatchi, Thurner, ESACCI, GEOCARBON, Saatchi2011 and USForest.

They disagree with each other by a median factor of 3.4× at these sites — Ozark ranges from 2.1 to 7.0 kg C m⁻² depending on which product you ask, central Europe from 3.7 to 15.6. So a comparison against any single product largely measures which product was chosen. The model is scored against the observational range instead.

A caution about tuning

With a 3.4× median spread between products, most of the apparent bias in any single comparison is observational rather than model error. Two further confounds push the same way: a 0.5° cell is roughly 50 km and mixes cropland and pasture into what the model treats as natural vegetation, and the products differ in whether they include coarse roots. Tuning allocation parameters to close a gap of this kind would be fitting the model to a dataset choice.

§7

What the residual actually is

The failures listed above share a description — dry, seasonal, herbaceous — but a description is not a diagnosis. Two questions decide what the residual means, and both have answers.

The error is entirely wood

Splitting the equilibrium into its pools is the first test. At Pampas the model carries 0.94 kg C m⁻² of leaf and root — inside the 0.3–1.0 range expected of temperate grassland — and then grows 10.6 kg C m⁻² of stem on top of it. The herbaceous part of the answer is right. Only the wood is wrong.

This settles a fair objection to §6. The observational products measure woody biomass, and over grassland they read near zero partly by construction, so scoring stem carbon against them might have been unfair to the model. It was not: total vegetation carbon is 12× the target as well, and every part of the excess is stem.

Climate explains the dry failures, but not these

Refined by the global map. This section was written from four sites, and it drew the boundary in the wrong place. Limiting woody allocation by mean annual precipitation — a climate variable, now in the model — fixed a large part of the residual, so “climate cannot help” was too strong a claim. What follows survives that correction intact, because it is about the wet failures, and those are exactly the ones water limitation leaves untouched: Pampas is still 4.6× over after the change.

The second test asks whether the model is misreading the climate. At these sites it is not. Pampas is warmer than central Europe and nearly as wet — 17.9 °C and 915 mm against 9.3 °C and 1056 mm — and central Europe is a genuine forest. Cerrado receives 1437 mm, which is climatically rainforest territory. Any model that predicts woody biomass from temperature, water and light should put trees at both.

They have no trees because of fire — Cerrado is the textbook fire-maintained savanna — and because of grazing and cultivation: Pampas, the Great Plains and the Songnen plain are among the most heavily converted landscapes on Earth. None of that is in the model, and none of it is inferable from climate.

Why two earlier negative results were really one

Mean annual precipitation could not separate grassland from forest, and neither could soil texture, organic matter or hydraulic conductivity. Read together with the above, that stops looking like two unlucky choices of predictor. The information that distinguishes these sites from forest is not in the climate and not in the soil, because it is in the disturbance regime — so no predictor drawn from either could have worked.

That yields a limitation precise enough to be useful rather than an apology: this model simulates climate-potential woody biomass, and is not given the disturbance information that determines where that potential is realised. It is a statement about a missing input, not a miscalibrated parameter, and it predicts exactly where the model will be wrong — which is a claim that can be checked rather than assumed.

§8

The same question, asked everywhere

Twenty columns test magnitude. They cannot test pattern, which is half of what this model was built to produce, and twenty sites chosen to span the biomes are not a sample of the world — the world is mostly not forest. So the pools were equilibrated at every land column of a global run: 22 420 columns, two minutes of compute, driven by a two-year global simulation at one degree.

Before reading anything into it, the map was checked against the twenty single-column results, which were produced long before any of this code existed. Seventeen of twenty agree within a factor of two. The three that do not track their own GPP differences almost exactly — a one-degree cell is not a point, and the Mojave cell runs 14 K warmer than the column, about two kilometres of terrain in a cell spanning Death Valley and the Sierra. Sampling, not arithmetic.

Where the error lives

Sorting the cells by what the observations say is there is what turned a single aggregate number into a diagnosis — and then into a change to the model. Limiting woody allocation by mean annual precipitation cut the mean bias several-fold in every product (ESACCI from +3.4 to +0.2 kg C m⁻²) while spatial correlation rose in all six, so the improvement was not bought by flattening the field.

This is the §7 diagnosis, no longer inferred from four sites but measured on twenty thousand cells. It also dissolves the two summary statistics above. More than half the vegetated land surface sits in the lowest two bins, so treeless ground dominates any area-weighted mean; and a correlation of 0.5 is what you get from a field that is right wherever there are trees and wrong wherever there are not.

What would falsify this

If the residual were a calibration problem — allocation too generous, turnover too slow — the ratio would sit roughly flat across the bins, or worsen where biomass is largest and the parameters matter most. It does the opposite, in three independently constructed products. A missing constraint on where wood can persist fits; a mis-set parameter does not. Adding the first such constraint, water, moved every bin and left the wet-savanna residual standing — which is what that reading predicted.

§9

How it is checked

The one-way coupling makes the central claim falsifiable, and it is tested rather than asserted. A battery run with the carbon model is diffed site-by-site against the same run without it: GPP and leaf area index come out bit-identical at all 20 sites under both LAI models — a relative difference of exactly zero, not merely within tolerance. The comparison tool was itself checked against a deliberately perturbed input, so a checker that always passes could not go unnoticed.

Alongside that, the test suite asserts carbon conservation to round-off from a non-trivial pool state; that autotrophic respiration is exactly the sum of its maintenance and growth parts; that respiration vanishes when every pool is empty, which the scheme it replaces structurally cannot do; and that litter reaching the soil integrates to the flux the canopy shed.

Several defects surfaced only because the whole battery was run rather than the sites that had already worked. Eleven of twenty columns returned non-finite pools on the first full sweep — every seasonal, cold or arid one — traced not to instability but to a canopy-temperature diagnostic that is deliberately masked where there is no leaf area. The driver record had inherited a plotting convention; the coupled model was unaffected throughout.
